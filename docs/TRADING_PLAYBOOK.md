## 1. Why Chart Patterns Work (and When They Don't)

Patterns are **not magic geometry**. They work because they are the *visible footprint* of three recurring phenomena:

1. **Supply & demand imbalance.** A tight range means sellers have been absorbed. A breakout on volume means demand has overwhelmed remaining supply. The pattern is the receipt.
2. **Behavioral finance / reflexivity.** Humans anchor on recent highs, feel FOMO at breakouts, freeze at support, and panic at breakdowns. Those reflexes repeat because the brain doesn't upgrade across cycles.
3. **Institutional accumulation / distribution.** Funds can't buy in one click — they accumulate across weeks, producing bases. They can't exit in one click — they distribute across tops. Patterns are their footprints.

**They fail when:**
- The broader market is in a downtrend (Stage 4). ~75% of stocks follow the index.
- Volume confirmation is missing. Low-volume breakouts fail ~65% of the time (O'Neil data).
- The base is too deep, too short, too loose, or "late-stage" (3rd/4th base after a long advance).
- Earnings or macro news invalidates the setup.
- You chase extended stocks more than 5% above the pivot.

> **Rule of thumb:** Patterns give an *edge*, not a *guarantee*. Edge = 45–55% win rate × 2–5R average winner. Without risk management the edge is worthless.

## 2. The Market Cycle — Weinstein's 4 Stages

Stan Weinstein's *Secrets for Profiting in Bull and Bear Markets* (1988) defines the single most important framework in swing / position trading:

| Stage | Name | 30-wk MA | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Basing / Accumulation | Flat, price oscillates around it | Watchlist, no buys yet |
| **2** | Advancing / Markup | Sloping up, price above | **BUY HERE** — 90% of gains live here |
| **3** | Topping / Distribution | Flattening, price choppy | Take profits, tighten stops |
| **4** | Declining / Markdown | Sloping down, price below | **Stay out.** Short for advanced traders |

- Use the **30-week SMA** (≈ 150-day) on weekly charts.
- Confirm with the RS line making new highs.
- Rule: **Only long Stage 2. Never long Stage 4.**

## 3. Core Concepts Every Trader Must Internalize

- **Trend is your friend.** Don't fight the primary direction (weekly / monthly).
- **Price > indicators.** Indicators lag; price and volume lead.
- **Volume confirms price.** Big up days on big volume = accumulation. Big up days on shrinking volume = exhaustion.
- **Tight action precedes big moves.** Volatility contraction (VCP) is Minervini's thesis.
- **Support / Resistance flip.** Broken resistance becomes support and vice-versa.
- **Time is a weapon.** The longer a base, the more explosive the breakout. The longer a top, the deeper the decline.
- **Asymmetry.** Cut losses at −7% to −8% (O'Neil); let winners run to +20%, +50%, +200%.

## 4. Continuation Patterns (the Money Makers)

### 4.1 Bull Flag / Flag-Pole
- **Anatomy:** Sharp vertical advance ("pole", +20% to +100% in days/weeks) → orderly downward-sloping pullback on decreasing volume ("flag") → breakout on volume.
- **Timeframe:** Flag = 3 to 20 trading days. Longer = pennant / base.
- **Entry:** Break of the upper flag trendline OR break of the flag high.
- **Stop:** Low of the flag, or ATR-based.
- **Target:** Measured move = length of the pole projected from breakout.
- **Expected R:R:** 3:1 to 10:1. Win rate ≈ 50–60% in trending markets.
- **Who uses it:** **Kristjan Kullamägi's entire system is built on episodic pivots + bull flags.** (Documented CAGR >100% for several years.)

### 4.2 Episodic Pivot (EP) — Kullamägi
- Stock gaps +10% to +40% on **massive** volume + **fundamental catalyst** (earnings beat, FDA, contract, guidance raise).
- Wait for the first red day (pullback).
- Enter on break of the first red day's high.
- Stop below low of day; hold while 10-EMA holds, trail with 20-EMA.
- These are the **highest-R trades** in the market when they work.

### 4.3 Pennant / Symmetrical Triangle
- Similar to flag but with converging trendlines (lower highs + higher lows).
- Breakout direction usually = prior trend direction (continuation bias).
- Volume must dry up during consolidation, surge on breakout.

### 4.4 Ascending Triangle
- Flat top (resistance) + rising bottoms. Classic accumulation signature.
- ~70% break upward in uptrends (Bulkowski statistics).

### 4.5 Channel (Parallel Up-Channel)
- Price oscillates between two parallel rising trendlines.
- **Buy lower rail**, sell upper rail, or break above upper rail = acceleration.
- Break of lower rail = trend change warning.

## 5. Base Patterns (Consolidation → Breakout)

These are **O'Neil / Minervini / Zanger** bread-and-butter.

### 5.1 Cup with Handle (O'Neil's favorite)
- **Cup:** Smooth U-shape (not a sharp V). Depth: 12–33% ideal (max ~50% in strong bulls).
- **Duration:** 7 weeks to 65 weeks (typical 3–6 months).
- **Handle:** Short downward drift in upper third of cup, 1–2 weeks, on dry volume.
- **Pivot:** High of the handle.
- **Buy:** Break of pivot on volume ≥ 40% above 50-day avg.
- **Stop:** 7–8% below pivot, or below handle low.
- **Target:** Base depth projected (often +20–30% in 4–8 weeks before rest).
- **Historical winners:** MSFT 1990, CSCO 1990, AAPL 2004, NVDA 2016 & 2023.

### 5.2 Flat Base
- Sideways consolidation after a prior advance. Depth < 15%, duration ≥ 5 weeks.
- Often forms as a *second-stage* base (higher probability than first).
- Minervini loves flat bases after VCP.

### 5.3 Double Bottom (with handle)
- W-shape. Second low slightly undercuts first (shakes out weak hands — O'Neil's "shakeout").
- Pivot = middle peak high.

### 5.4 VCP — Volatility Contraction Pattern (Minervini)
- Series of **progressively tighter** pullbacks (e.g., −25%, then −15%, then −8%, then −4%).
- Volume dries up in each contraction (supply exhausted).
- Breakout on **massive** volume.
- Minervini calls this the "footprint of institutional accumulation."
- Look for **2T to 4T** (2 to 4 tightenings). 3T is classic.

### 5.5 High Tight Flag (HTF) — rare but explosive
- Stock rises 90–120% in 4–8 weeks → consolidates within ~25% for 3–5 weeks → breaks out.
- One of the highest-performing patterns historically (Zanger, O'Neil).
- Extremely rare — maybe 10–20 per year in US markets.

### 5.6 Power Earnings Gap (Minervini)
- Gap-up on earnings on 5×+ average volume, closes near high.
- Buy break of gap-day high, or on pullback to 10-EMA.

## 6. Reversal Patterns

### 6.1 Head & Shoulders (Top)
- Left shoulder, higher head, lower right shoulder, neckline.
- Break of neckline on volume = sell / short.
- Target = head-to-neckline distance projected below neckline.
- **Inverted H&S** at bottoms = long setup.

### 6.2 Double Top / Triple Top
- Two / three peaks at similar highs; break below intervening low confirms.

### 6.3 Rounding Bottom / Saucer
- Slow, gradual base over months — Stage 1 → Stage 2 transition.

### 6.4 Key Reversal Bar
- New high, closes near low, on huge volume = potential top.
- New low, closes near high, on huge volume = potential bottom (capitulation).

## 8. Entry Techniques

### 8.1 Pivot Breakout Entry
- Buy on break of the pattern high, ideally intraday on volume pickup.
- Risk: entering extended if the breakout runs before you click.

### 8.2 Pullback Entry (preferred by risk-averse)
- Wait for breakout → pullback to pivot or 10 / 20-EMA → buy bounce.
- Better R:R, lower win rate (some setups never pull back).
- **Kullamägi uses 10-EMA pullback entries aggressively.**

### 8.3 Early Entry (Minervini's "pocket pivot")
- Buy *inside* the base on a bullish day where up-volume exceeds the largest down-volume of the last 10 days.
- Only if stock is in a proper structure (above 50-day MA, RS line rising).

### 8.4 First Green Day / First Red Day (Kullamägi)
- After parabolic drop: **first green day** close = swing-low long entry.
- After parabolic rally + pullback: **first red day** high = breakout short OR continuation long next day.

### 8.5 ORB — Opening Range Breakout (intraday)
- Mark the first 5 / 15 / 30-min range. Trade the break with volume.
- Works best on news / gap days with RS.

## 9. Relative Strength, ADR, Liquidity — the Screening Filters

### 9.1 Relative Strength (RS)
- **IBD RS Rating ≥ 85** (Minervini: ≥ 90, often ≥ 97).
- RS line making new 52-week highs **before** price = leadership signal.
- In weak markets, RS leaders are the *only* things worth buying.

### 9.2 ADR — Average Daily Range %
- `ADR% = avg( (high-low)/close ) over 20 days × 100`.
- **Kullamägi's filter: ADR% ≥ 5%.** Low-ADR stocks can't deliver swing R:R.
- Too high (>15%) = whippy, hard to hold.

### 9.3 Liquidity
- Avg $ volume > $2M/day (small accounts), > $20M/day (large accounts).
- Price > $10 (avoid penny-stock manipulation) — flexible for Indian mid-caps.

### 9.4 Trend Template (Minervini's 8 criteria)
A stock must meet **ALL** before you look at it:
1. Price > 150-day MA AND > 200-day MA.
2. 150-day MA > 200-day MA.
3. 200-day MA trending up ≥ 1 month (preferably 4–5 months).
4. 50-day MA > 150-day MA > 200-day MA.
5. Price > 50-day MA.
6. Price ≥ 25% above 52-week low.
7. Price within 25% of 52-week high.
8. RS Rating ≥ 70 (ideally 80+).

## 10. Market Timing — Bottoms, Tops, Follow-Through Days

### 10.1 Identifying a Market Bottom (O'Neil FTD)
- After a downtrend, wait for an attempted rally (Day 1).
- On **Day 4 to Day 10** of the rally, look for a **Follow-Through Day**: index up ≥ 1.5–2% on volume higher than prior day.
- FTDs have preceded every major bull market since 1900 (though ~30% fail within weeks).
- Only start buying breakouts AFTER an FTD.

### 10.2 Signs of a Market Top
- **Distribution days:** index down ≥ 0.2% on higher volume. 5+ in 25 days = warning.
- Leaders breaking down, laggards (defensives, utilities) leading.
- Number of stocks making new highs shrinking while index makes new highs (breadth divergence).
- IPOs spiking then collapsing.
- Margin debt at extremes, euphoric sentiment (AAII, put/call, VIX complacency).
- Yield curve inversion + Fed tightening — macro overlay.

### 10.3 Signs of a Market Bottom
- Capitulation volume (highest-volume sell day, panic close).
- Breadth washout — % stocks above 50-day MA < 10%.
- VIX spike > 35–40 and starting to roll over.
- Successful retest of low on lower volume.
- FTD.

## 11. Risk Management & Position Sizing

> **"The first job is to protect capital. The second job is to make it grow."** — Van Tharp

### 11.1 Per-Trade Risk
- Risk **0.25% – 1.0%** of account per trade. Beginners: 0.5% max.
- Define risk BEFORE entry: `Risk$ = (Entry − Stop) × Shares`.

### 11.2 Position Size Formula
```
Shares = (Account × Risk%) / (Entry − Stop)
```
Example: $100k × 0.5% = $500 risk. Entry $50, Stop $47 → $3 risk → 166 shares = $8,300 position (8.3% of account).

### 11.3 Total Exposure Rules
- Max **5–10 positions** concurrently (focus beats diversification — Livermore, Zanger).
- Minervini: "concentrated portfolio of best-in-class." 4–8 names.
- Max **25% in any single stock** after a winner runs.
- Reduce exposure when index below 50-day MA.

### 11.4 Stop Loss Rules
- **Hard stop:** 5–8% below entry (O'Neil's rule).
- **Structural stop:** Below pattern low / swing low / 10-EMA.
- **ATR stop:** Entry − (1.5 to 2 × ATR).
- **Time stop:** If no progress in 5–10 days, exit (position should work immediately).

## 12. Trade Management & Exits

### 12.1 When It Works
- **First profit target:** +1R to +2R — sell 25–33% to de-risk (optional but reduces emotional load).
- **Trail with 10-EMA** (Kullamägi) for fast movers.
- **Trail with 20-EMA or 21-day MA** for normal swings.
- **Trail with 50-day MA** for position trades (Minervini).
- **Sell on violations:** close below 10 / 20 / 50 MA (depending on time horizon).

### 12.2 When It Doesn't Work
- Stop hit → exit. **No negotiation. No averaging down.** Livermore's #1 rule.
- Failed breakout (closes back in base) → exit even before stop hits.
- Distribution day on your stock (gap down big volume) → exit.

### 12.3 Climax / Exhaustion Top Signs (Minervini's sell rules)
- Largest one-day % gain of the entire move.
- Exhaustion gap on massive volume.
- 3+ consecutive up days after a long run, each with widening range.
- Stock extended > 70% above 200-day MA.
- Break of upper channel line after parabolic move.

### 12.4 Scaling Out
- 1/3 at +5× risk, 1/3 at +10× risk, let last 1/3 ride with trailing stop. (Zanger style.)

## 13. Trader Ideologies Distilled

### **Jesse Livermore** (*Reminiscences of a Stock Operator*)
- Trade the **line of least resistance** — buy strong, sell weak.
- "It's never your thinking that makes big money; it's the sitting."
- **Pivotal points** = breakouts from consolidation.
- Never average down. Always cut losses fast.
- Don't trade when you have no setup — "the market will pay you to wait."

### **William J. O'Neil** (*How to Make Money in Stocks*) — **CAN SLIM**
- **C**urrent quarterly EPS up ≥ 25% YoY.
- **A**nnual EPS up ≥ 25% for 3 years.
- **N**ew product / management / highs.
- **S**upply and demand — low float, buybacks.
- **L**eader, not laggard — RS ≥ 85.
- **I**nstitutional sponsorship — rising fund ownership.
- **M**arket direction — only buy in confirmed uptrend (FTD).

### **Mark Minervini** (*Trade Like a Stock Market Wizard*, *Think & Trade Like a Champion*)
- **SEPA** (Specific Entry Point Analysis): Trend + fundamentals + catalyst + entry + risk.
- Trend Template + VCP + Pocket Pivots.
- "Risk is the only thing you can control. Focus on risk; profits take care of themselves."
- Two-time US Investing Champion (1997: 241% return; 2021: 334%).

### **Dan Zanger** (world-record holder — $10,775 → $18M in <2 years, 1998–2000)
- Trade only the **strongest 1% of stocks** during runaway bull cycles.
- Pattern focus: **Cup & Handle, Bull Flag, Channels, Pennants, High Tight Flag.**
- Volume must be ≥ 2× 30-day average on breakout.
- Use **multiple time frames** (weekly for trend, daily for entry, intraday for precision).
- Sell into strength; trail tight once parabolic.

### **Kristjan Kullamägi** (Swedish trader, documented 10,000%+ returns)
- **Episodic Pivots** + **Bull Flags** + **Parabolic Shorts**.
- ADR ≥ 5%, liquidity filter, RS filter.
- Enter on break of first red day's high after a big move.
- Trail with 10 or 20 EMA.
- "I only take A+ setups. I'd rather do nothing than force a B setup."
- Scans global markets (US, SG, SE). All his material is free on YouTube.

### **Stan Weinstein**
- 4-Stage cycle + 30-week MA.
- Volume thrust confirmation on breakouts.
- Relative Strength line (vs. S&P) must be rising.
- Only long Stage 2; only short Stage 4.

### **Nicolas Darvas** (*How I Made $2,000,000 in the Stock Market*, 1960s)
- **Darvas Box Theory:** stocks trade in boxes; buy break of top, stop below bottom.
- Trade only **new 52-week highs**.
- "I became a mental chartist." Simplicity over noise.

### **Peter Lynch** (*One Up on Wall Street*)
- Invest in what you know; scuttlebutt research.
- **GARP** — Growth at a Reasonable Price (PEG < 1).
- 10-baggers hide in overlooked small caps.

### **Paul Tudor Jones**
- "Losers average losers."
- 5:1 R:R minimum.
- "The secret is defense, defense, defense."

### **Prateek Bhonde** (Power of Stocks — Indian markets)
- Intraday: trade the **first 15-min candle break** with volume; target 1:2 or 1:3 R:R.
- Swing: Trendline breaks, inside-bar breakouts on daily, BTST on strong close near high.
- Strict risk: 1% per trade; never add to losers.
- Focus on index futures + F&O large caps for liquidity.
- Option-selling framework for sideways markets (credit spreads, iron condors around key supports/resistances).

### **Vivek Gadodia / ProRSI / Indian quant school**
- Quantified momentum + relative strength ranking across Nifty 500.
- Monthly rebalance of top 20 by composite score.
- Backtested CAGR ~25–30% with drawdowns ~25%.

### **Mark Douglas** (*Trading in the Zone*, *The Disciplined Trader*)
- Markets are fundamentally **uncertain**; each trade's outcome is unknowable.
- Think in **probabilities over a series of trades**, not the outcome of any single one.
- The **5 Fundamental Truths**:
  1. Anything can happen.
  2. You don't need to know what will happen next to make money.
  3. Wins and losses are randomly distributed across any given edge.
  4. An edge is merely a higher probability of one outcome.
  5. Every moment in the market is unique.
- The **7 Principles of Consistency** — internalize these as beliefs, not slogans.

### **Van Tharp** (*Trade Your Way to Financial Freedom*)
- **Expectancy** = (Win% × AvgWin) − (Loss% × AvgLoss). Must be > 0.
- **R-multiples** — think in units of risk, not dollars.
- Position sizing is 90% of performance variance between traders.
- Edge comes from *you* (psychology) × system × position sizing.

### **Alexander Elder** (*Trading for a Living*)
- **Triple Screen:** weekly trend → daily oscillator → intraday trigger.
- **2% / 6% rule:** max 2% per trade, max 6% total open risk.

### **Brent Penfold**
- "Universal Principles": trend-following + cut losses + let winners run + risk management + psychology = any timeframe works if disciplined.

## 14. Trading Psychology — Mark Douglas & Beyond

### 14.1 The Four Trading Fears
1. **Fear of being wrong** → hesitation, missed entries.
2. **Fear of losing money** → premature exits, moving stops away.
3. **Fear of missing out (FOMO)** → chasing, over-sizing.
4. **Fear of leaving money on the table** → giving back profits, not selling.

**Cure:** Define system rules in writing. Execute *exactly*. Measure process, not outcome.

### 14.2 The Probability Mindset
- Stop thinking "Will THIS trade work?" Start thinking "Over the next 100 trades, will my edge pay?"
- Accept that **any single trade can lose** — that's not failure, it's sampling.

### 14.3 Rituals That Work
- **Pre-market routine:** index check, watchlist, no-news-no-trade filter, set alerts.
- **Trade journal:** every trade, screenshot, setup type, R multiple, emotional state, lesson.
- **Weekend review:** tag winners/losers by setup, find the 20% of setups making 80% of money — cut the rest.
- **Daily P&L blind:** don't watch money tick; watch chart levels.

### 14.4 Ego Management
- "Be right" = broke. "Make money" = rich. These are different goals.
- Your opinion doesn't matter. The tape is the truth.
- Revenge trading is the #1 account killer. Walk away after 2 consecutive losses.

### 14.5 Position-Size Down When Hurting
- After a drawdown > 10%: cut size by 50% until account recovers 5%. Mechanical rule. No exceptions.

## 15. A Unified Checklist

Before pulling the trigger on ANY trade, answer ALL:

**Market (M):**
- [ ] Index above 50-day MA? (Or valid FTD?)
- [ ] Leadership broad (many stocks breaking out)?

**Stock (S):**
- [ ] Passes Minervini Trend Template (8 criteria)?
- [ ] RS Rating ≥ 85 (Indian equivalent: top decile by 3 / 6 / 12-month return)?
- [ ] ADR% ≥ 3% (swing) or ≥ 5% (Kullamägi-style)?
- [ ] Liquidity sufficient (< 1% of ADV for your size)?
- [ ] Earnings not within next 5 trading days?

**Setup (E):**
- [ ] Clear, named pattern (VCP / cup-handle / flag / EP / flat base)?
- [ ] Volume dry in contraction, surge on breakout?
- [ ] Not extended > 5% above pivot?

**Trade (T):**
- [ ] Entry, stop, first target, final target all written?
- [ ] Risk ≤ 1% of account?
- [ ] Position size calculated (not guessed)?
- [ ] R:R ≥ 3:1?

**Self (Y):**
- [ ] Emotionally neutral? (Not revenge-trading, not euphoric)
- [ ] Followed every rule of my playbook?

If any box is unchecked → **no trade.**

- Most profitable traders compound 15–25% with <15% drawdown. That still beats 95% of funds.

| **55%** | **+0.10** | **+0.38** | **+0.65** | **+0.93** | **+1.20** | **+1.75** | **+2.30** |
| **60%** | **+0.20** | **+0.50** | **+0.80** | **+1.10** | **+1.40** | **+2.00** | **+2.60** |
| **65%** | **+0.30** | **+0.63** | **+0.95** | **+1.28** | **+1.60** | **+2.25** | **+2.90** |
| **70%** | **+0.40** | **+0.75** | **+1.10** | **+1.45** | **+1.80** | **+2.50** | **+3.20** |

**Reading this table:**
- **Red (negative) = you lose money long-term.** No matter how good your pattern recognition feels.
- **The "break-even" diagonal** runs from 50% @ 1R → 33% @ 2R → 25% @ 3R → 20% @ 4R.
- **Realistic swing traders** live at **40–50% WR × 2.5–3.5R** → expectancy **+0.4R to +1.0R**.
- **Kullamägi-style episodic pivots** claim **30–40% WR × 5–10R** → expectancy **+0.8R to +3.0R** (but rare setups).

### 16.5 Per-100-Trades Dollar Outcome (Risking 1R = $500 on $100k)

What does a given edge actually *earn* you over a 100-trade sample?

| System | Win% | R:R | Expectancy | **Net R / 100 trades** | **$ on $100k (1R=$500)** | **% return** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Breakeven | 50% | 1.0 | 0.00R | 0R | $0 | 0% |
| Weak | 40% | 1.5 | 0.00R | 0R | $0 | 0% |
| Coin-flip + edge | 50% | 2.0 | +0.50R | +50R | **+$25,000** | **+25%** |
| Kullamägi-lite | 40% | 3.0 | +0.60R | +60R | **+$30,000** | **+30%** |
| Minervini swing | 45% | 3.0 | +0.80R | +80R | **+$40,000** | **+40%** |
| Elite EP trader | 35% | 5.0 | +1.10R | +110R | **+$55,000** | **+55%** |

> **Key insight:** A 35% WR with 5R setups *crushes* a 70% WR with 0.5R setups. **Size of winner vs. loser beats frequency of winning.** This is why pros hunt asymmetry, not accuracy.

### 16.6 Drawdown → Recovery Math (The Asymmetry of Losses)

A loss requires a **disproportionately larger gain** to recover. This is the most underestimated number in trading.

| Drawdown | **Required gain to recover** | Trades @ +1R (risking 1%) needed | Comment |
|---|---|---|---|
| −5% | +5.3% | 5 R | Trivial |
| −10% | +11.1% | 11 R | Manageable |
| −15% | +17.6% | 18 R | Painful |
| −20% | **+25.0%** | 25 R | Size-down threshold |
| −25% | +33.3% | 33 R | Confidence damaged |
| −30% | +42.9% | 43 R | Career wound |
| −40% | +66.7% | 67 R | Most never recover |
| **−50%** | **+100%** | **100 R** | Psychology broken |
| −60% | +150% | 150 R | Almost impossible |
| −75% | +300% | 300 R | Dead account |
| −90% | +900% | 900 R | Game over |

**Rule:** **Never let a drawdown exceed 20%.** Beyond that, the math and the psychology both compound against you.

### 16.7 Position Sizing Impact & Risk of Ruin

What happens if you take **N consecutive losers** at various per-trade risk levels?

| Per-trade risk | After 5 losses | After 10 losses | After 20 losses | **Max losses before −50% DD** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0.25%** | −1.2% | −2.5% | −4.9% | ~275 losses |
| **0.5%** | −2.5% | −4.9% | −9.6% | ~140 losses |
| **1.0%** | −4.9% | −9.6% | −18.2% | ~70 losses |
| **2.0%** | −9.6% | −18.3% | −33.2% | **~35 losses** |
| **3.0%** | −14.1% | −26.3% | −45.6% | **~23 losses** |
| **5.0%** | −22.6% | −40.1% | −64.2% | **~14 losses** |
| **10.0%** | −41.0% | −65.1% | −87.8% | **~7 losses** |
| **20.0%** | −67.2% | −89.3% | −98.8% | **~4 losses** |

> **Risk of Ruin (RoR)** at 50% WR: risking 2%/trade → RoR ~0.1%. Risking 10%/trade → RoR **~60%**. Risking 20%/trade → RoR **~99%**. The math is brutal and non-negotiable.

**Minervini, Kullamägi, O'Neil all risk 0.25–1.25% per trade.** Not because they're timid — because they've done the math.

### 16.8 Consecutive Loss Probability (You WILL Hit a Cold Streak)

At any given win rate, the probability of hitting **N losses in a row** over a 100-trade sample is uncomfortably high.

| Win Rate | P(5 in a row) | P(7 in a row) | P(10 in a row) | **Expected max losing streak in 100 trades** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30% | **~99.9%** | ~99% | ~87% | **10–12** |
| 40% | ~99% | ~92% | ~60% | **8–10** |
| 50% | ~96% | ~72% | ~31% | **6–7** |
| 55% | ~88% | ~55% | ~16% | **5–6** |
| 60% | ~73% | ~36% | ~7% | **4–5** |
| 65% | ~54% | ~20% | ~2% | **4** |
| 70% | ~33% | ~9% | <1% | **3** |

**Implication:** If your system is 40% WR (normal for breakouts), **expect a 10-loser streak every ~100 trades.** That's **−10R** = **−10% drawdown at 1% risk**, or **−50% at 5% risk.** Plan for it mentally and mechanically *before* it happens.

### 16.10 Kelly Criterion — Optimal Bet Sizing

Kelly formula: `Kelly% = W − [(1 − W) / R]` where W = win rate, R = avg win / avg loss.

| Win% | R:R | **Full Kelly %** | **Half-Kelly (recommended)** | **Quarter-Kelly (safe)** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 40% | 2.0 | 10% | 5% | 2.5% |
| 45% | 2.0 | 17.5% | 8.8% | 4.4% |
| 50% | 2.0 | 25% | 12.5% | 6.3% |
| 40% | 3.0 | 20% | 10% | 5% |
| 50% | 3.0 | 33% | 16.5% | **8.3%** |
| 40% | 5.0 | 28% | 14% | 7% |
| 50% | 5.0 | 40% | 20% | 10% |

**Why NOT full Kelly:** drawdowns at full Kelly are brutal (50%+ DDs are normal). Pros run **Half-Kelly or less**. Even Half-Kelly is aggressive — most champions actually run **Quarter-Kelly = ~1–2% per trade** on typical setups.

**Sanity check:** If your Kelly % is negative → **your system has no edge. Stop trading it.**

## 17. Common Failure Modes

1. **No written plan.** Impulse trading guarantees eventual ruin.
2. **Over-leveraging.** One 30% drawdown on leverage = game over.
3. **Moving stops.** Converts a 1R loss into account destruction.
4. **Averaging down.** Compounds losers.
5. **Trading earnings blindly.** Earnings = lottery, not edge.
6. **Low-ADR stocks.** Can't pay R:R.
7. **Trading in Stage 4 markets.** 75% of stocks fall with the tape.
8. **Pattern hunting without market filter.** A good setup in a bad market = bad setup.
9. **Too many positions.** Unable to manage; attention diluted.
10. **Tying self-worth to P&L.** Psychological death spiral.
11. **Not journaling.** No feedback loop = no improvement.
12. **Over-optimization.** Curve-fit backtests die in live markets.
