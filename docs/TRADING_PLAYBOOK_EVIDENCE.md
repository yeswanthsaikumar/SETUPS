## 1. Academic Proof That Momentum / Breakouts Work

This is the single most-studied anomaly in finance. It survives **peer review, out-of-sample tests, and 200+ years of data**.

### 1.1 Jegadeesh & Titman (1993)
- **Paper:** *"Returns to Buying Winners and Selling Losers: Implications for Stock Market Efficiency"*, Journal of Finance.
- **Finding:** A strategy that buys the top-decile 6-month winners and sells bottom-decile losers earns **~12% per year** above market, 1965–1989.
- **Why it matters:** First rigorous academic confirmation of what O'Neil, Zanger, Minervini do intuitively.

### 1.2 Carhart (1997) — 4-Factor Model
- Added **MOM (momentum)** as a factor alongside Fama-French's market, size, value.
- Momentum factor averaged **~8–10% annualized premium** over 70+ years.

### 1.3 Asness, Moskowitz, Pedersen (2013) — *"Value and Momentum Everywhere"*
- Tested momentum across **8 asset classes and 40+ years**.
- Sharpe ratio of momentum ≈ **0.5** across stocks, bonds, currencies, commodities.
- Combining value + momentum → Sharpe **0.8+**, drawdowns halved.

### 1.4 Geczy & Samonov (2016) — *"Two Centuries of Multi-Asset Momentum"*
- 215 years of data (1800–2014).
- Momentum persists across **every decade** except ephemeral crashes (1932, 2009 momentum crash).
- Cross-sectional equity momentum = **~7%/yr premium** over 2 centuries.

### 1.5 AQR Momentum ETFs (live, real-money evidence)
- QMOM, MTUM — live since 2013. Have outperformed S&P 500 by 1–3%/yr on average over the 10-yr window, with higher vol.
- Not a holy grail, but confirms the factor is real post-publication.

### 1.6 Blitz, Huij, Martens (2011) — *"Residual Momentum"*
- Stripping out beta, size, value from momentum returns → cleaner alpha, **Sharpe ~0.8**.
- Breakout / Stage-2 systems implicitly trade residual momentum.

> **Bottom line:** Momentum / trend-following has **5+ decades of academic validation** and **200+ years of empirical persistence**. This is not theory — it is the single best-documented anomaly in finance.

## 3. O'Neil / IBD Research — CAN SLIM & Market Timing

William O'Neil's firm studied **the top 600 performing stocks from 1880–2010** (every major winner for 130 years). Findings published in *How to Make Money in Stocks*:

### 3.1 Characteristics of Every Major Winner
- **95%+** broke out from a proper base (cup-handle, flat base, double bottom, HTF).
- **Median base depth:** 25–35%. Base duration: **7+ weeks**.
- **Average gain from pivot to top:** **100%–200%** over 6–18 months.
- **Earnings acceleration:** Quarterly EPS up ≥ 25% YoY in 95% of cases, often 50%+.
- **RS Rating:** Median **87** at breakout.
- **Group Rank:** Top 20% of industries in 80% of winners.

### 3.2 Sell Rules (O'Neil Research)
- **7–8% stop:** Statistical analysis showed stocks that fall 7–8% below a proper pivot have only a **~25% chance of recovering to breakeven within 3 months**.
- **+20% rule:** Most CAN SLIM winners experienced a first profit of +20% within 8 weeks of pivot. Holding past that requires explicit trend signals.

### 3.3 Follow-Through Day (FTD) Track Record
- **IBD study (1900–2020):** Every major bull market start was preceded by a valid FTD.
- **False signal rate:** ~30% — i.e., 70% of FTDs lead to 3-month+ advances.
- **Average gain in 12 months after a valid FTD:** S&P 500 +15% to +25%.
- **Distribution-day count > 6 in 25 days:** 80%+ hit rate for a 10%+ correction in next 3 months.

## 7. Stage Analysis — Backtests of Weinstein's Framework

### 7.1 Meb Faber (2007) — *"A Quantitative Approach to Tactical Asset Allocation"*
- Simple rule: **hold S&P when price > 10-month SMA, cash otherwise**.
- 1901–2020: same CAGR as buy-and-hold (~9.5%) with **half the max drawdown** (~50% → ~23%).
- Sharpe improved from 0.4 → 0.6.
- **Validates "only long Stage 2" concept mathematically.**

### 7.2 Trend + Momentum (Quantpedia, various)
- Going long only stocks above 200-day MA and in top-quintile momentum:
- **CAGR: 15–20% (US, 1963–2020)** vs. 10% S&P.
- **Drawdown: 25–35%** vs. 55% S&P.
- **Sharpe: 0.8–1.0** vs. 0.5.

### 7.3 Wilshire / Ned Davis Research
- Stocks in Stage 2 (price above 30-week MA, MA rising) outperform the market by ~8%/yr.
- Stocks in Stage 4 underperform by ~11%/yr.
- **Spread: ~19%/yr between long-leaders and long-laggards.** This is the core momentum premium.

## 9. Position Sizing / Expectancy — Empirical Math

### 9.1 Ralph Vince / Van Tharp Research
- Simulated 100 traders with **identical 60% win rate / 2R avg winner system** but different position sizing.
- **Result:** after 100 trades, returns ranged from −40% to +1,200% depending on risk %.
- **Optimal fixed-fractional risk: ~2% per trade** (Kelly-lite).
- Risking > 5% → negative expectancy in finite samples despite positive edge.

### 9.2 The Expectancy Formula (Empirical)
`E = (Win% × AvgWin) − (Loss% × AvgLoss)`

Realistic breakout system (example from Kullamägi's own disclosed journal, 2017–2019):
- Win rate: **~33%**
- Avg winner: **+24%** (6.0R)
- Avg loser: **−4%** (1.0R)
- Expectancy: `(0.33 × 6) − (0.67 × 1) = 1.98 − 0.67 = 1.31R per trade`
- At 100 trades/yr risking 0.5%/trade: **annual return ≈ 65%** (before compounding & losing streaks).

**This is the math behind why a 33% win rate beats a 70% win rate with bad R:R.**

### 9.3 Losing Streak Probability (Binomial Math)
With a 40% win rate, probability of:
- 5 losses in a row: **7.8%** (will happen multiple times per year)
- 7 losses in a row: **2.8%** (once every ~2 years)
- 10 losses in a row: **0.6%** (once every ~5 years)

**Implication:** Your risk per trade must survive a 10-loss streak. 10 × 1% = 10% drawdown. 10 × 3% = 30% drawdown (requires +43% to recover). **This is why 1% per trade is the ceiling, not the target.**

## 12. Failure Statistics — Why Most Traders Lose

### 12.1 Brazilian BM&FBovespa Study (Chague, De-Losso, Giovannetti, 2020)
- Studied **19,646 retail day traders, 2013–2015**.
- **97% lost money.**
- Only **0.4%** earned more than a bank teller.
- **Only 3 individuals out of 19,646** earned > $54/day average.

### 12.2 Taiwan Day Trader Study (Barber, Lee, Liu, Odean, 2014)
- 15 years of day-trader data.
- **Less than 1%** of day traders earn predictably positive returns after fees.

### 12.3 ESMA (European Securities Authority) Retail CFD Study 2018
- **74–89% of retail CFD accounts lose money** (depending on broker).
- This figure must now by law be disclosed by every EU broker on every ad.

### 12.4 SEBI Study on Indian F&O Traders (2023)
- 2021–2022 data: **89% of individual F&O traders lost money**.
- **Average loss: ₹1.1 lakh.**
- Top 1% earned ~₹4 lakh; bottom 89% lost ~₹50k each.
- **Overall traders net lost ₹45,000 crore to profitable counterparties.**

### 12.5 The Pattern Behind Failure
- **Over-sizing:** >5% risk per trade.
- **No stops or moving stops:** turning losers into disasters.
- **Revenge trading after losses.**
- **Trading in bear markets with long strategies.**
- **No written plan / no journal.**

## 13. What The Data Says You Should Actually Do

Compressing 100+ years of evidence into bullet points:

### 13.1 Statistically High-Probability Actions
1. **Only trade in confirmed uptrends** (index > 200-DMA, or post-FTD). Expected +8–10%/yr vs. −5–10%/yr in downtrends.
2. **Trade momentum leaders** (top decile RS). +7% to +10% premium over 100+ years.
3. **Use clean patterns**: Cup-handle, flat base, VCP, HTF, bull flag, episodic pivot. Failure rates 3–10% in bulls.
4. **Demand volume confirmation**: 40%+ above 50-day avg on breakout day.
5. **Risk 0.25–1% per trade.** Historical expectancy math says anything higher eventually fails.
6. **Cut at 5–8% loss.** O'Neil research: recovery probability drops sharply past 8%.
7. **Let winners run until they violate 10/20/50 MA** (depending on horizon).
8. **Size down when drawn down.** Statistical edge erodes in tilt.

### 13.2 Statistically Low-Probability Actions (Avoid)
1. **Averaging down.** Converts losers into account-killers in ~15–20% of cases.
2. **Trading bear markets long.** 75% of stocks fall; pattern failure rates 2–3× normal.
3. **Chasing 10%+ above pivot.** Post-extension win rate drops from ~55% → ~35%.
4. **Low-ADR / low-liquidity names.** Can't deliver R:R; slippage erodes edge.
5. **Holding through earnings without plan.** Post-earnings gaps cause 40% of large single-day losses.
6. **Unfiltered day trading.** 89–97% failure rate across every major study.
7. **Leverage > 2×.** Kelly math shows blow-up probability approaches 100% over many trades.
