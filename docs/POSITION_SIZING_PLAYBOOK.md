# Position Sizing Playbook — How Much to Risk on Every Trade

> **The single skill that separates traders who survive from traders who blow up.** Most people study setups for years and lose money. They study sizing for a weekend and become consistent. This guide is your sizing system from 0% to 100% — built so you can read it once a week, internalize it, and never blow up again.

## A Story Before the Rules

Two traders walk into the same market.

Both of them spot the same setup — a tight VCP base in a strong leadership stock, breaking out on volume. They both buy at the pivot. They both place the stop where the chart says it belongs. They both are right about the trade.

One walks out a few months later with a 3% account gain.
The other walks out with a 28% account loss.

What changed?

**Trader A** sized the trade so that, if his stop hit, he'd lose **0.5% of his account**.
**Trader B** "felt strongly" about the setup. He sized so that if it failed he'd lose **8% of his account**. He had the exact same setup. The exact same chart. The exact same stop. But his sizing meant the loss was 16x larger.

Now imagine that scenario across 30 trades a year. Trader A's edge compounds. Trader B's account dies — not because his analysis was wrong, but because his sizing was reckless.

> **Setup quality determines your win rate. Position size determines your survival.**

This playbook is built around one core idea:

> **Sizing isn't a "how much can I make" question. It's a "how much can I afford to be wrong" question.**

If you internalize that single sentence, everything else in this guide is just mechanics.

## 1. The Core Sizing Equation

There is exactly one formula you need:

```
Position Size (₹) = Risk Per Trade (₹) ÷ Risk Per Share (₹)
```

Where:
- **Risk Per Trade (₹)** = Account × Risk %
- **Risk Per Share (₹)** = Entry − Stop

That's it. Memorize this. Everything else flows from it.

### Worked example

You have a ₹10,00,000 account. You decide to risk **0.75%** per trade — that's ₹7,500.
You spot a Bull Flag breakout at ₹462. The flag low is ₹452. So your stop is ₹452 → risk per share = ₹10.

```
Position Size = ₹7,500 ÷ ₹10 = 750 shares
Capital deployed = 750 × ₹462 = ₹3,46,500
Capital deployed % = 34.6%
```

You're using **34.6% of your account capital** but only **risking 0.75%** of your account. That's the difference between *capital deployed* and *risk taken* — and 95% of beginners confuse the two.

> **Capital deployed ≠ risk. Risk is the distance from entry to stop, multiplied by share count.**

## 2. The Risk-Per-Trade Tiers (Pick Your Number)

There is no single "right" risk %. There are **three tiers** based on experience and conviction:

| Tier | Risk Per Trade | Use When |
|---|---|---|
| **Conservative** | 0.25% – 0.5% | New trader, drawdown period, choppy market, low conviction |
| **Standard** | 0.5% – 1.0% | Normal market conditions, A-grade setups, tested system |
| **Aggressive** | 1.0% – 2.0% | Strong trending market, exceptional setup, multiple confirmations, < 5% account drawdown |

**Rule:** Beginners default to **0.5%**. Intermediate to **0.75%**. Advanced to **1.0%**. Never start above 1%.

### Why 1% is the natural ceiling
At 1% risk per trade and a typical 50% win rate at 2R reward, your expectancy is +0.5R per trade. A 10-trade losing streak (which happens to everyone) costs 10%. A 20-trade losing streak (rare but real) costs 20%. You can recover from both.

At 3% risk per trade, the same 20-trade streak puts you down 60%. That requires a +150% gain to come back. **You will never come back.** That's how accounts die.

### The math everyone ignores

| Drawdown | Gain needed to recover |
|---|---|
| -10% | +11% |
| -20% | +25% |
| -30% | +43% |
| -40% | +67% |
| -50% | +100% |
| -60% | +150% |
| -70% | +233% |
| -80% | +400% |

**Translation:** Below -30% drawdown, the math gets brutal. Below -50%, recovery is statistically improbable in a normal lifetime. **Sizing exists to keep you above -20%.**

## 3. The Five-Layer Sizing System (The Professional's Real Method)

Pros don't just multiply account by 1%. They run every trade through five filters that **scale risk up or down** based on context.

### Layer 1: Account Risk (The Base)
- Default: 0.75% of account.
- That's your starting number for every trade, before any adjustments.

### Layer 2: Market Regime Multiplier
- **Strong uptrend (above 50d/200d MAs, breadth healthy):** 1.0× → use full risk
- **Neutral (sideways, mixed signals):** 0.5× → halve risk
- **Weak / correction (below 50d MA, breadth deteriorating):** 0.25× → quarter risk
- **Bear (below 200d MA):** 0× → no new long trades, focus on cash

### Layer 3: Setup Quality Multiplier
- **A+ setup** (multi-timeframe alignment, RS leader, classic pattern, dry volume, fresh base): 1.0×
- **A setup** (most criteria met, one minor flaw): 0.75×
- **B setup** (concept is right but pattern loose or RS mediocre): 0.5×
- **C setup or worse:** **don't take it.** No size adjustment fixes a bad trade.

### Layer 4: Personal Streak Multiplier
- After **3 wins in a row:** 1.0× (don't get cocky, but don't shrink either)
- After **3 losses in a row:** 0.5× (your read is off; protect capital while you recalibrate)
- After **5 losses in a row:** 0.25× and **mandatory journal review** before next trade
- After **drawdown > 7% from peak:** 0.5× until you regain the high
- After **drawdown > 12%:** stop trading. Review every losing trade. Resume at 0.25×.

### Layer 5: Position Concentration Cap
- **Max 5 open positions** at standard size (this is a soft rule — see §6).
- **No more than 25% of account in any single position** (capital deployed, not risk).
- **No more than 40% of account in one sector** (correlated drawdown risk).

### The combined formula

```
Final Risk = Account × Base% × Regime × Setup × Streak
```

**Example:** ₹10L account, base 0.75%, neutral regime (0.5×), A setup (1.0×), no streak adjustment (1.0×).

```
Final Risk = 10,00,000 × 0.0075 × 0.5 × 1.0 × 1.0 = ₹3,750
```

You're risking ₹3,750 instead of ₹7,500 — half size — because the regime says caution. **This is what protects you when "good setups" stop working.**

## 4. Starter Position vs Pyramiding (How Pros Build Big Winners)

A common mistake: putting on full size at the breakout candle and then having no plan.

The professional method:

### Starter position (50% of full size)
- Enter at the breakout / trigger.
- Use the standard stop.
- This is your *probe* — proof that the trade is working before you commit fully.

### Pyramid #1 (+25%, taking position to 75%)
- Add when price moves **1R in your favor** AND closes constructively.
- Raise the stop on the entire position to the original entry (break-even).
- Now you're risking only the original 0.5R of base risk.

### Pyramid #2 (+25%, taking position to full size)
- Add when price moves **2R in your favor** AND a clean inside-day or new tight pivot forms.
- Raise stop to **+0.5R from initial entry** (now you've locked partial profit on the entire trade).
- Trail the rest under each higher low.

> **Never pyramid into a losing position. Pyramiding is a reward you give a trade for proving itself.**

This system means your full-sized positions are mathematically correlated with your winners — and your half-sized positions take the losses. That asymmetry is where consistent profits come from.

## 5. The "Heat Map" — Total Open Risk at Any Moment

Open positions all have stops. The total risk if every stop hit simultaneously = **portfolio heat**.

### The heat ceiling
- **Max portfolio heat: 4% of account.**
- That means if you're already running 4 positions × 1% each, you cannot open a 5th until one position moves to break-even (releasing its risk).

### Daily heat audit (60 seconds before market open)
1. Sum `(entry − current stop) × shares` for every open position.
2. Express as % of account.
3. If > 4%, **don't take new trades.** Manage what you have first.

### Why this rule exists
Markets correlate during selloffs. Your 5 "uncorrelated" tech leaders all gap down together on a Fed surprise. Heat caps protect you from days you didn't see coming.

## 6. Position Count vs Account Size (The Beginner Trap)

| Account | Recommended max open positions |
|---|---|
| < ₹2L | 2–3 |
| ₹2L – ₹5L | 3–5 |
| ₹5L – ₹15L | 5–7 |
| ₹15L – ₹50L | 5–10 |
| > ₹50L | 8–12 |

**Why beginners overdiversify:** They feel safer holding 15 small positions. In reality, they can't manage 15 stops, 15 plans, 15 invalidations. They miss exits, they miss adds, their attention dilutes.

> **Concentration with strict stops beats diversification with loose attention.** Pick fewer trades, manage them ruthlessly.

## 7. Real Examples — Full Trade Plans

### Example A: A+ setup in a strong market
- Account: ₹10L, base 0.75%, regime 1.0×, setup 1.0× → risk ₹7,500
- Entry: ₹500, stop: ₹485, risk/share = ₹15
- Shares: 500 → capital deployed ₹2,50,000 (25% of account)
- T1 at ₹530 (+2R) — sell 1/3, raise stop to ₹500
- Pyramid #1 at ₹530 with +25% size, stop now ₹510
- Trail under each higher low. Max-gain projection: ₹570 (cup depth).

### Example B: Mediocre setup in a weak market
- Account: ₹10L, base 0.75%, regime 0.25×, setup 0.5× → risk ₹937
- Entry: ₹240, stop: ₹230, risk/share = ₹10
- Shares: **94** → capital deployed ₹22,560 (2.3% of account)
- That's a tiny position. **Good.** The market doesn't reward this trade right now, so you bring an umbrella, not a parachute.

### Example C: After a 4-loss streak
- Account: ₹10L, base 0.75%, streak 0.5× → risk ₹3,750
- This is the size that lets you keep trading without making your hole worse.
- Once you put up 2 winners back-to-back at this reduced size, return to base.

## 8. Daily, Weekly, Monthly Habits

### Daily (before market open — 3 minutes)
- [ ] Check current portfolio heat (% of account at risk).
- [ ] Apply regime multiplier to today's risk allowance.
- [ ] Confirm no position exceeds 25% of account.
- [ ] Confirm sector concentration < 40%.
- [ ] Note today's max risk per new trade.

### Weekly (Sunday — 15 minutes)
- [ ] Review all closed trades by R-multiple (winners vs losers).
- [ ] Flag any trades sized outside the system rules.
- [ ] Update streak counter (W/L last 5).
- [ ] Update peak-equity drawdown level → set base risk for next week.
- [ ] Re-read sections 2 and 3 of this playbook.

### Monthly (last weekend — 30 minutes)
- [ ] Total wins, losses, average R per trade, expectancy.
- [ ] Identify your "best size" trades vs your "worst size" trades.
- [ ] Was every loss inside 1.0R? If not, *why?*
- [ ] Cross-check: did pyramiding ever happen on a losing trade? (If yes, root-cause why.)
- [ ] Was max heat ever breached? (If yes, what discipline failed?)
- [ ] Re-read this entire playbook.

## 9. The Sizing Mistakes That Kill Accounts

The seven deadly sizing sins:

1. **Sizing based on conviction, not stop distance.** "I really like this one" is not a sizing input.
2. **Wider stops to "give it room"** — same risk, smaller share count. You're disguising the same loss.
3. **Pyramiding into losers** — averaging down. The loss compounds, the math collapses.
4. **All-in on "the perfect setup."** There is no setup so good it justifies bypassing sizing rules. The market is humbler than you.
5. **Skipping the regime multiplier.** Bull market sizing in a bear market = career death.
6. **Ignoring portfolio heat.** Five 1% trades on the same correlated theme is one 5% trade pretending to be diversified.
7. **Increasing size after a hot streak.** This is the classic blow-up moment. Stay disciplined.

Read this list every Monday. Tape it to your monitor.

## 10. The One-Page Sizing Cheat Sheet

```
Step 1 — Decide base risk per trade (default: 0.75% of account).
Step 2 — Apply regime multiplier (1.0 / 0.5 / 0.25 / 0).
Step 3 — Apply setup quality multiplier (1.0 / 0.75 / 0.5).
Step 4 — Apply streak multiplier (1.0 / 0.5 / 0.25).
Step 5 — Risk Per Trade ₹ = Account × all multipliers.
Step 6 — Shares = Risk ₹ ÷ (Entry − Stop).
Step 7 — Confirm portfolio heat stays ≤ 4%.
Step 8 — Enter starter (50% size). Pyramid only on profit.
```
